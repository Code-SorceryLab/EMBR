  \caption{%
    The five signals of the EMBR composite score. The score is a weighted sum of these terms, so
    zeroing a weight disables a signal: that is exactly how the RQ3 ablations and both baselines are
    expressed, as weight maps over one scorer rather than as duplicated retrieval code. Formula
    sketches and groundings follow the design specification; this table is authored specification,
    not measured output.
  }
  \label{tab:embr-signals}
  \footnotesize
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
  \begin{tabular}{llp{0.15\linewidth}p{0.25\linewidth}}
    \toprule
    Signal & Formula (sketch) & Grounding & What it captures \\
    \midrule
    Recency & $\lambda^{\Delta t}$ & Park et al. 2023; MemoryBank & How long ago the memory formed. Ordinary chatter fades from retrieval while nothing is ever deleted from the store. \\
    Affect intensity & $|v|\cdot a$ & Cahill and McGaugh 1998 & How strongly the moment was felt, on the arousal-modulated consolidation finding that emotional events are remembered better than neutral ones. \\
    Event-type gate & $\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{plot\,beat}]\cdot g(\mathrm{trust})$ & novel (this thesis) & Whether the memory is a promise, gift, or betrayal, gated by how far the NPC trusts the speaker, so plot beats outrank small talk. \\
    Hybrid relevance & $\gamma\,\mathrm{BM25} + (1-\gamma)\cos(e_m,e_q)$ & standard hybrid retrieval & Whether the memory is about what was just said, mixing lexical overlap with embedding similarity so neither rare wording nor paraphrase is missed. \\
    Mood congruence & $\cos((v_m,a_m),(v_s,a_s))$ & Bower 1981; Emotional RAG & Whether the memory's affect matches the NPC's current mood, the mood-congruent recall effect. This is the term RQ1 measures. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
